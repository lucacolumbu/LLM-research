# Literature check: mechanism-informed data selection

Date: 2026-09-11. Scope: data selection / attribution scored against circuits or mechanisms; induction-head training dynamics vs. data; compression/LZ links to induction; data-selection baselines; synthetic IOI testbeds. Every URL below was returned by a live search or fetched directly. "Verified" means the arXiv abstract page (or HTML) was fetched and read; "snippet" means only a search-result summary was seen.

## Verdict

The idea is **partly taken, but the specific framing is open**. *Mechanistic Data Attribution* (Chen, Luo, Pan; ICML 2026 oral, arXiv 2601.21996) already scores training samples by their influence on a circuit-level probe (the Olsson prefix-matching score for induction heads) using EK-FAC on Pythia 14M-160M, and shows that deleting or duplicating the top-influence samples modulates induction-head emergence and ICL; its headline finding (repetitive structured data such as LaTeX/XML is the catalyst) is exactly what a zipper score would pick out. Three further 2026 papers use SAE features or discovered circuits to select or generate *fine-tuning* data (Translation Switch, AAAI 2026; SAMS, Aug 2026; Concept Influence, Feb 2026). What no paper found does is (a) greedy selection of a minimal *pretraining* subset judged by whether target circuits form and full-data performance is matched, (b) a cost-tiered proxy ladder with LZ77 compression gain as the cheap stand-in for a circuit-level influence score, or (c) any use of IOI-style circuits as a selection target. The project must be positioned as "MDA turned from post-hoc attribution into forward selection, with compression proxies", and must engage Sabry & Belz (2025), who show that data interventions that amplify induction signatures do not necessarily make the mechanism load-bearing.

## Directly overlapping work

### 1. Mechanistic Data Attribution: Tracing the Training Origins of Interpretable LLM Units
Jianhui Chen, Yuzhang Luo, Liangming Pan. 2026. ICML 2026 (oral). arXiv:2601.21996. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21996 (verified; HTML v2 read for method)

Defines a probing function f_probe on an evaluation set D_probe for an interpretable unit (for induction heads: the prefix-matching score) and computes per-sample influence of training data on that probe using EK-FAC restricted to the unit's parameter subspace, on Pythia 14M/31M/70M/160M within the induction-emergence checkpoint window. Deletion (masking gradients of top-influence samples) delays induction-head formation and degrades ICL; duplication accelerates it; random interventions do nothing. Influence follows a power law (top 10% of samples carry ~50% of cumulative influence); the top samples are repetitive structured text (LaTeX, XML, Base64, enumeration lists), and even semantically meaningless repetitive strings outrank clean prose. A "mechanistic data augmentation" pipeline distils patterns from the top-2000 samples into procedural generators. **Overlap:** this is Tier-3 (circuit-probe-scored influence) done properly, and it establishes the empirical premise that repetition-heavy documents drive induction heads. **Differences:** it is attribution and deletion/augmentation of an existing run, not selection of a minimal subset trained from scratch; no compression proxy; no greedy coverage objective; no IOI or multi-circuit target; no comparison of selected-subset vs full-data downstream performance. Must be cited as the closest prior work.

### 2. Finding the Translation Switch: Discovering and Exploiting the Task-Initiation Features in LLMs
Xinwei Wu et al. 2026. AAAI 2026. arXiv:2601.11019. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11019 (verified)

Identifies SAE "translation initiation" features, validates them causally, then proposes "mechanistic data selection" for *fine-tuning*: prioritise "mechanistically hard" samples that fail to activate the features. Improves data efficiency and reduces hallucination. **Overlap:** explicitly frames selection by an internal mechanism rather than loss. **Differences:** SFT not pretraining; feature activation on the sample, not the sample's effect on circuit formation; selects hard samples, not circuit-forming ones.

### 3. Mechanistic Circuit Identification for Controllable Data Generation
Nakyung Lee, Sangwoo Hong, Jungwoo Lee. 2026. arXiv:2608.24065. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24065 (verified; HTML read)

Uses EAP-IG on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct to find circuits associated with three data-utility scores (AUM learnability, EL2N challenge, GradAlign), then steers *synthetic* data generation via activation addition and attention steering through those circuits; candidates are filtered by teacher-forced log-likelihood "under target circuits"; a stage-aware scheduler (SAMS) mixes data across training phases for fine-tuning a 0.5B student on SciQ-style QA. **Overlap:** circuits used as the interface for data curation; stage-aware scheduling. **Differences:** circuits of *utility metrics*, not of a capability; generation not selection; fine-tuning not pretraining.

### 4. Concept Influence: Leveraging Interpretability to Improve Performance and Efficiency in Training Data Attribution
Matthew Kowal, Goncalo Paulo, Louis Jaburi, Tom Tseng, Lev McKinney, Stefan Heimersheim, Aaron Tucker, Adam Gleave, Kellin Pelrine. 2026. arXiv:2602.14869. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14869 (verified)

Attributes model behaviour to semantic directions (linear probes, SAE features) instead of to test examples; shows probe-based attribution is a first-order approximation that is an order of magnitude faster. **Overlap:** attribution target is an interpretable internal direction, and it introduces exactly the cheap-proxy-vs-full-influence tiering logic the project relies on. **Differences:** features, not circuits; attribution, not selection.

## Adjacent work to position against

### Angle 1: circuit/mechanism x data attribution, influence, curation

5. **Symbolic Mechanistic Data Attribution.** Reza Habibi, Darian Lee, Magy Seif El-Nasr. 2026. arXiv:2606.29171. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29171 (verified). Ridge regression over SAE features distils a symbolic policy (refusal in Llama-3.2-3B); each SFT pair's influence is decomposed into feature-activation and output-probability pathways. Its own abstract states that "existing data attribution methods can identify which training examples build specific mechanistic circuits", citing MDA as settled ground. Position: behavioural policy, SFT, not circuit formation in pretraining.

6. **Influence Dynamics and Stagewise Data Attribution.** Jin Hwa Lee, Matthew Smith, Maxwell Adam, Jesse Hoogland (Timaeus). 2025. arXiv:2510.12071. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12071 (verified). Influence is non-monotonic over training; sign flips and peaks align with developmental transitions including induction-circuit formation (per search snippet from the Timaeus page https://timaeus.co/research/2025-10-05-influence-dynamics). Companion: *Bayesian Influence Functions for Hessian-Free Data Attribution*, arXiv:2509.26544, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26544 (snippet). Position: a per-document score is stage-dependent, so a static Tier-1/Tier-2 score needs to be argued for at the circuit-formation window specifically; Timaeus's LLC could serve as an alternative "circuit formed?" signal. See also Hoogland et al., *The Developmental Landscape of In-Context Learning*, arXiv:2402.02364 (snippet).

7. **Scalable Influence and Fact Tracing for LLM Pretraining (TrackStar).** Tyler Chang, Dheeraj Rajagopal, Tolga Bolukbasi, Lucas Dixon, Ian Tenney. 2024. arXiv:2410.17413. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17413 (verified). Gradient-based attribution over 8B params / 160B tokens; influential examples often reinforce priors on relation types and names rather than containing the fact. Together with Grosse et al., *Studying LLM Generalization with Influence Functions*, arXiv:2308.03296, https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03296 (snippet), this is the loss-targeted TDA baseline the project's Tier-3 must be contrasted with.

8. **Capability Provenance in Language Models: A Case Study in Social Reasoning.** Glenn Matlin, ..., Louis Jaburi, Lucia Quirke, Stella Biderman, Mark Riedl. COLM 2026. arXiv:2606.19625. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19625 (verified). EleutherAI-orbit TDA mapping which corpus regions support social vs STEM reasoning in OLMo3-7B, validated by targeted unlearning. Tooling: *Bergson: An Open Source Library for Data Attribution*, Quirke, Jaburi, ..., Biderman, Belrose, arXiv:2606.11660, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11660 (verified), which implements EK-FAC, TrackStar, MAGIC, SOURCE. Position: capability-level, not circuit-level; no retraining-from-scratch on selected data. Bergson is the obvious library for Tier-3.

9. **Data Attribution of Emergent Misalignment with Persona Features.** Clemens Vetter, David Kaczer, Lucie Flek, Florian Mai. 2026. arXiv:2608.11025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11025 (verified). SAE model-diffing finds persona features amplified by misaligned fine-tuning, then attributes those features to 1M pretraining documents. Position: feature-targeted attribution exists; circuit-targeted forward selection does not. Related, not content-verified (OpenReview bot check): Jaburi, *Mitigating Emergent Misalignment with Data Attribution*, https://openreview.net/forum?id=fQvVV6UN4p.

10. **Understanding In-Context Learning via Supportive Pretraining Data.** Xiaochuang Han, Daniel Simig, Todor Mihaylov, Yulia Tsvetkov, Asli Celikyilmaz, Tianlu Wang. ACL 2023. arXiv:2306.15091. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15091 (verified). Iterative gradient-based selection of a small pretraining subset that supports ICL; continued pretraining on it improves ICL by up to 18%; supportive data has more long-tail tokens and lower long-range information gain. Position: the closest pre-2025 analogue of "select pretraining data for ICL", but scored against ICL task loss, not a circuit, and applied as continued pretraining, not minimal-subset-from-scratch.

### Angle 2: induction-head formation as a function of data

11. **In-context Learning and Induction Heads.** Catherine Olsson, Nelson Elhage, Neel Nanda, et al. 2022. Transformer Circuits Thread / arXiv:2209.11895. https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895 (snippet). Source of the prefix-matching and copying scores that MDA and this project use as probes.

12. **Data Distributional Properties Drive Emergent In-Context Learning in Transformers.** Stephanie Chan, Adam Santoro, Andrew Lampinen, Jane Wang, Aaditya Singh, Pierre Richemond, Jay McClelland, Felix Hill. NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2205.05055. https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05055 (snippet). Burstiness, many rare classes, and Zipfian marginals drive ICL. Position: the distributional knobs a document-level selector implicitly optimises; a zipper score is a per-document burstiness proxy.

13. **The Mechanistic Basis of Data Dependence and Abrupt Learning in an In-Context Classification Task.** Gautam Reddy. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2312.03002. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03002 (snippet). Two-parameter phenomenological induction-head model; abrupt emergence from sequentially learned nested logits; data dependence of the transition.

14. **What Needs to Go Right for an Induction Head?** Aaditya Singh, Ted Moskovitz, Felix Hill, Stephanie Chan, Andrew Saxe. ICML 2024. arXiv:2404.07129. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07129 (verified). Optogenetics-style clamping identifies three subcircuits whose data-dependent formation timing explains the phase change. Related: Singh et al., *The Transient Nature of Emergent ICL*, arXiv:2311.08360, and *Strategy Coopetition*, arXiv:2503.05631 (snippets), which warn that induction/ICL can be transient under continued training, a risk for any selected subset.

15. **The Evolution of Statistical Induction Heads: In-Context Learning Markov Chains.** Benjamin Edelman, Ezra Edelman, Surbhi Goel, Eran Malach, Nikolaos Tsilivis. NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2402.11004. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11004 (snippet). Unigram-then-bigram phases on synthetic Markov-chain data; a controllable-entropy testbed.

16. **Birth of a Transformer: A Memory Viewpoint.** Alberto Bietti, Vivien Cabannes, Diane Bouchacourt, Herve Jegou, Leon Bottou. NeurIPS 2023 spotlight. arXiv:2306.00802. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00802 (snippet). Global vs in-context bigram synthetic data; fast global-bigram learning, slow induction-head development; associative-memory view of weights.

17. **Predicting the Emergence of Induction Heads in Language Model Pretraining.** Tatsuya Aoyama, Ethan Wilcox, Nathan Schneider. ICML 2026. arXiv:2511.16893. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16893 (verified). Batch size x context size predicts the emergence point independent of model size; surface bigram repetition frequency and reliability define a decision boundary for IH formation on natural and synthetic data. Position: the most direct surface-statistic predictor of induction formation; the project's Tier-1 should be benchmarked against bigram-repetition frequency/reliability, not only gzip. No compression/LZ in the abstract.

18. **From Shortcut to Induction Head: How Data Diversity Shapes Algorithm Selection in Transformers.** Kawata et al. NeurIPS 2025. arXiv:2512.18634. https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18634 (snippet). Low "max-sum" ratio of trigger-to-trigger distances yields a true induction head; high ratio yields a positional shortcut; derives the cost-optimal pretraining distribution. Position: selection by repetition alone can induce the wrong (shortcut) mechanism; distance diversity matters. Related: Zucchet, d'Angelo, Lampinen, Chan, *The Emergence of Sparse Attention: Impact of Data Distribution and Benefits of Repetition*, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2505.17863, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17863 (verified): repetition accelerates emergence with power-law timing.

19. **In-Context Learning Without Copying.** Kerem Sahin, Sheridan Feucht, Adam Belfki, Jannik Brinkmann, Aaron Mueller, David Bau, Chris Wendler. 2025. arXiv:2511.05743. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05743 (verified). "Hapax" masks the loss on tokens predictable by induction (31.7% of tokens); models form fewer/weaker induction heads yet beat baseline on 13/21 abstractive ICL tasks. Position: the inverse experiment (suppress the circuit via the data/loss); also the natural negative control for the project.

20. **Induction Signatures Are Not Enough: A Matched-Compute Study of Load-Bearing Structure in ICL.** Mohammed Sabry, Anya Belz. DATA-FM @ ICLR 2026. arXiv:2509.22947. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22947 (verified). "Bi-Induct" interleaves copy snippets into pretraining (130M-1B); induction-head activity rises but few-shot generalisation does not improve, and ablating top induction heads hurts natural-only models more. Position: the strongest caution for this project; the evaluation must show selected data produces *causally necessary* circuits, not just higher induction scores.

21. **LLM Circuit Analyses Are Consistent Across Training and Scale.** Curt Tigges, Michael Hanna, Qinan Yu, Stella Biderman. NeurIPS 2024. arXiv:2407.10827. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10827 (verified abstract; IOI-tracking detail from snippet). Tracks IOI and successor circuits across 300B Pythia tokens and 70M-2.8B; algorithms stable, components drift. Position: provides IOI-circuit formation timing in Pythia to compare against.

### Angle 3: compression / LZ and induction

22. **Language Modeling Is Compression.** Gregoire Deletang, Anian Ruoss, ..., Marcus Hutter, Joel Veness. ICLR 2024. arXiv:2309.10668. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668 (snippet). Compression rate improves with context length because models learn statistics in-context; gzip/LZMA as baselines. No induction-head analysis.

23. **Induction Heads Interpolate N-Grams.** Francesco D'Angelo, Oguz Kaan Yuksel, Swathi Shree Narashiman, Nicolas Flammarion. ICML 2026. arXiv:2607.02800. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02800 (verified). Induction circuits implement Jelinek-Mercer-style interpolation over context orders plus Dirichlet-style pseudo-counts. Position: the closest formal link between induction heads and classical sequence predictors; the "LZ77 is a hand-coded induction head" claim should be stated relative to this (n-gram smoothing) rather than asserted. No paper found that states the LZ77-induction equivalence explicitly; the universality side is covered by Sagan & Weissman, *A Family of LZ78-based Universal Sequential Probability Assignments*, IEEE Trans. IT, arXiv:2410.06589, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06589 (verified).

24. **gzip Predicts Data-dependent Scaling Laws.** Rohan Pandey. 2024. arXiv:2405.16684. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16684 (verified). Dataset gzip compressibility modulates compute-optimal scaling on PCFG data. Position: compressibility as a dataset-level property; the project uses it per-document and ties it to a mechanism.

25. **ZIP-FIT: Embedding-Free Data Selection via Compression-Based Alignment.** Elyas Obbad, Iddah Mlauzi, Brando Miranda, Rylan Schaeffer, Kamal Obbad, Suhana Bedi, Sanmi Koyejo. 2024/25. arXiv:2410.18194. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18194 (verified). gzip-based alignment between candidate and target data; reaches target cross-entropy up to 85% faster. Position: compression-based *selection* exists, but as target-distribution matching (like DSIR), not as a mechanism proxy.

26. **Entropy Law: The Story Behind Data Compression and LLM Performance.** Mingjia Yin et al. 2024. arXiv:2407.06645. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06645 (verified). Performance is negatively correlated with training-set compression ratio; "ZIP" greedily selects low-compression-ratio (diverse) subsets. Position: opposite sign to the zipper score (Entropy Law wants *incompressible* data; the project wants LZ-compressible, repetition-rich data to build induction). The project must reconcile the two, plausibly via stage (repetition early, diversity later) or mixture.

### Angle 4: data-selection baselines

27. **DSIR: Data Selection for Language Models via Importance Resampling.** Sang Michael Xie, Shibani Santurkar, Tengyu Ma, Percy Liang. NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2302.03169. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03169 (snippet). Hashed n-gram importance weights toward a target distribution. Survey of the field: Albalak et al., *A Survey on Data Selection for Language Models*, TMLR 2024, arXiv:2402.16827, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16827 (snippet).

28. **DoReMi: Optimizing Data Mixtures Speeds Up Language Model Pretraining.** Sang Michael Xie et al. NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2305.10429. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10429 (snippet). Group-DRO on a 280M proxy sets domain weights for an 8B model. Position: domain-level reweighting baseline.

29. **Prioritized Training on Points that are Learnable, Worth Learning, and Not Yet Learnt (RHO-LOSS).** Soren Mindermann et al. ICML 2022. arXiv:2206.07137. https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07137 (snippet). Reducible holdout loss selection. Position: the project's Tier-3 is RHO-LOSS with a circuit-specific probe set in place of a generic holdout set. Model-aware extension: MATES, Yu, Das, Xiong, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.06046, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06046 (verified), which trains a small influence model on locally probed oracle signals.

30. **Perplexed by Perplexity: Perplexity-Based Data Pruning With Small Reference Models.** Zachary Ankner, Cody Blakeney, Kartik Sreenivasan, Max Marion, Matthew Leavitt, Mansheej Paul. 2024. arXiv:2405.20541. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20541 (snippet). A 125M reference model prunes data for a 3B model. Position: Tier-2 baseline; the project's variant conditions the reference model on the already-selected set (sequential/greedy).

31. **Deduplicating Training Data Makes Language Models Better.** Katherine Lee, Daphne Ippolito, Andrew Nystrom, Chiyuan Zhang, Douglas Eck, Chris Callison-Burch, Nicholas Carlini. ACL 2022. arXiv:2107.06499. https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06499 (snippet). Position: dedup removes cross-document repetition; the zipper score rewards within-document repetition; the project should separate the two.

32. **LESS: Selecting Influential Data for Targeted Instruction Tuning.** Mengzhou Xia, Sadhika Malladi, Suchin Gururangan, Sanjeev Arora, Danqi Chen. ICML 2024. arXiv:2402.04333. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04333 (snippet). Low-rank gradient similarity to a few-shot target set; 5% selected beats full data. Position: gradient-based targeted selection; the project swaps the target set for a circuit probe set. Cost framing: Yin & Rush, *Compute-Constrained Data Selection*, arXiv:2410.16208, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16208 (snippet), find perplexity and gradient selection are only compute-optimal at 5x/10x train-to-selector ratios, which motivates the cheap Tier-1 proxy.

### Angle 5: synthetic IOI / circuit-formation testbeds

33. **Emergence of Minimal Circuits for Indirect Object Identification in Attention-Only Transformers.** Rabin Adhikari. ACL 2026 SRW. arXiv:2510.25013. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25013 (verified). Symbolic 6-token IOI task trained from scratch; a 1-layer, 2-head attention-only model reaches 100% with additive and contrastive subcircuits. Position: the only from-scratch synthetic IOI training study found; no explicit name-pool-size or entropy knobs reported in the abstract (not verified in the body).

34. **Interpretability in the Wild: A Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 Small.** Kevin Wang, Alexandre Variengien, Arthur Conmy, Buck Shlegeris, Jacob Steinhardt. ICLR 2023. arXiv:2211.00593. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593 (URL from search; content from prior knowledge). Defines the IOI task, templates, and name pools the project would reuse as a probe set.

No paper was found that trains on a synthetic "who did what" corpus with a tunable name-pool entropy specifically to study *data-driven* IOI-circuit formation; the Markov-chain (Edelman), bigram (Bietti), and trigger-copy (Kawata) synthetic families are the nearest controllable-entropy testbeds, all for induction rather than IOI.

## Gaps and opportunities

- **Forward selection, not attribution.** MDA scores influence on an already-trained run and intervenes by deletion/duplication; nobody has used a circuit-probe score to greedily assemble a minimal pretraining subset from scratch and reported subset-vs-full-data parity. This is the project's core claim and it appears untried.
- **Compression as a mechanism proxy.** gzip/LZ has been used for dataset-level scaling (Pandey), target alignment (ZIP-FIT), and diversity (Entropy Law), but never as a per-document proxy for "will this document build an induction head". Note the sign conflict with Entropy Law and the fact that Aoyama et al.'s bigram repetition frequency/reliability is an existing, cheap, mechanism-motivated competitor that Tier-1 must beat or match.
- **The LZ77-induction-head equivalence is not in the literature.** D'Angelo et al. (n-gram interpolation) and Sagan & Weissman (LZ78 universal prediction) are the nearest support; stating the equivalence carefully (LZ77 match-copy vs soft prefix-matching with smoothing) would be a contribution in itself.
- **Multi-circuit targets.** All data-vs-mechanism work targets induction heads (or SAE features). Scoring against IOI or a set of circuits, and studying interference between circuit-specific subsets, is untouched.
- **Load-bearing validation.** Sabry & Belz show amplified induction signatures need not be causally necessary. A selection method that verifies via ablation that the induced circuit carries the capability would answer the strongest objection in advance.
- **Stagewise scoring.** Lee et al. (Timaeus) show influence changes sign across developmental stages; a selection curriculum keyed to the induction-formation window (repetition-rich early, diverse later) is suggested by the union of MDA, Entropy Law, and Kawata but not tested.
- **Shortcut risk.** Kawata et al. show repetition without trigger-distance diversity yields positional shortcuts; a zipper score should be paired with a distance-diversity check.
- **Proxy-ladder calibration.** Concept Influence and Yin & Rush make the cost argument for cheap proxies, but no work calibrates a cheap statistic against circuit-level influence; reporting rank correlation of Tier-1/2 with Tier-3 on MDA-style top-influence samples is a clean, publishable sub-result.
